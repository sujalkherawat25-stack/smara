Smara for Indian MSMEs: what to sell, to whom, and in what order

A business and product audit, written in plain language, with the plan from 30 Banswara users to 3 paying ones, a grant, and then 100+ users.

Short answer. Indian owners pay for one thing: money and time they can see. So Smara should not be sold as "an AI assistant". It should be sold as "your udhaar comes back faster, and your khata stays clean, without you chasing anyone".

  1. Now: sell vasooli (collections) plus clean statements, on WhatsApp. It is built, safe and measurable in rupees.
  2. Next: order-to-cash on WhatsApp: order, then bill, then UPI link, then payment matched, then reminder.
  3. Then: the Smara Network. When a buyer and a seller both use Smara, their agents confirm ledgers, send quotes and purchase orders, and track dispatch, and each owner taps to approve.
  4. Later: answering IndiaMART leads, agent-to-agent price deals inside limits the owner sets, and early-payment financing referrals.

Do not lead with the B2B agent marketplace. It is the right big vision, but it needs both sides on Smara first. Collections gets you the first side.

1. How messy it is today

For a typical Banswara wholesaler, distributor or manufacturer, the money cycle runs on memory, phone calls and a diary or Tally:

What the numbers say

  • Stuck money is huge. A government-linked estimate puts MSME money locked in delayed payments at about ₹7.34 lakh crore (Free Press Journal). Even the cases formally filed on the government's Samadhaan portal leave about ₹21–22 thousand crore still unpaid, and one study found only about 22% of portal cases resolved (Crisil, Zenodo study). Owners do not go to the government for this. They chase it themselves.
  • The law helps manufacturers, not traders. Since April 2024, buyers lose a tax deduction if they pay a registered micro or small supplier after 45 days (15 without a written agreement). Several sources say this mostly does not protect pure traders (Business Standard, Busy). So a small manufacturer can say "you must pay me in 45 days", and a trader has to keep chasing. Both need follow-up.
  • Tally is the backbone. About one in four MSMEs use Tally (Mint). Smara already reads Tally and Excel exports, so owners do not have to switch software. That matters a lot in India.
  • Invoice finance barely reaches small firms. TReDS (selling invoices for early cash) is growing, but under 1% of registered MSMEs are on it (Credable, M1xchange). Clean, verified receivables data from Smara is what a lender needs later.
  • IndiaMART leads are a race. An enquiry is usually shared with several suppliers, so the one who replies first and best wins, and sellers complain about weak or resold leads (Garnet, IndiaMART help). Paid sellers can pull their leads by API (IndiaMART Pull API), so an agent can answer in minutes.
  • GST matching hurts buyers. A buyer's input tax credit depends on the supplier filing correctly, so every month there are mismatches to chase (Tally). This is a B2B problem by nature: both sides must agree. That is a strong reason for the Smara Network later.

Several figures come from news and vendor sources that disagree on dates; treat them as direction, not exact values.

Continuum (MemoryOS): what to do with it

Continuum is a serious memory engine (vector search, knowledge graph, decay and evidence), and it is live. Indian MSMEs will never buy "agent memory". Indian developer teams are also slow buyers. So:

  • Use it as Smara's moat, not as a product to sell in India. Customer by customer, it is what makes Smara remember "Ravi always pays after the 10th", "Sharma disputes freight", or "this buyer bargains 3%". Competitors' reminder apps do not have this.
  • Keep the open Community Edition alive cheaply for global developers. It is good proof of technical depth for grant reviewers and investors.
  • Do not spend your selling hours on it until Smara has 100+ users. Revisit then, perhaps as "memory for Indian enterprise agents" or for global buyers.

3. Where the real value is, in rupees

Owners decide in rupees. Here is the kind of math to show a Banswara distributor (illustrative; replace with each shop's real numbers in week 1):

Value to the owner (per month)

  • ₹40 lakh outstanding with customers.
  • If Smara's steady follow-up brings payment in 10 days earlier on average, that frees about ₹13,000 a month of interest at 12% (40L × 12% × 10/365), or that much less on the OD account.
  • Plus 1 hour a day of the owner's or accountant's time not spent calling and writing statements.
  • Plus fewer disputes, because every promise and claim is on record.

Cost to run (per shop per month, estimate)

  • Model: a simple question used about 9,300 tokens on live logs. An active shop might use 10–15 million tokens a month. At ₹10–40 per million tokens, that is ₹100–600. Sarvam is free for now; measure with the usage report.
  • WhatsApp: replies inside 24 hours are free. A reminder template costs about ₹0.12–0.15 each, so 300 reminders is about ₹40 (Robylon, Meta).
  • Server: one VM handles 30 shops, so about ₹50–100 per shop.
  • Total: roughly ₹200–750 per active shop.

Pricing to test with power users: ₹999 a month, or "₹1,999 a month, or 1% of extra money collected, whichever is lower". Ask for the first payment only after the owner has seen at least one month of collections. Indians pay for results they already saw, not for promises.

4. The product stages

Why this order

  • Stage 1 sells on day one. One owner gets value alone. No network is needed.
  • Stage 2 keeps them daily. Orders and bills happen every day, but dues only weekly. Daily use is what makes someone pay.
  • Stage 3 is the growth engine. Every Smara user has 20–200 customers and suppliers. When Smara sends "Ravi Traders has shared their ledger with you, tap to confirm", the other business sees Smara working before they sign up. That is your free distribution inside Banswara.
  • Stage 4 needs trust and data. Agents bargaining on price only works once owners trust Smara's judgement and both sides have history.

5. The B2B agent channel, designed to be safe

Your idea, "my company's agent deals with your company's agent", is right. Gartner expects most B2B buying to pass through AI agents within a few years (Keelvar summary). For Indian SMEs it must work like this:

The first network feature to build: "Khata Milao" (ledger match)

  • A seller's Smara sends the buyer a ledger confirmation: "As per our books you owe ₹1,85,750, made of these 3 bills".
  • The buyer's Smara compares it with the buyer's own books. If they match, the buyer taps "Confirm". If not, it lists exactly which bill differs.
  • Today this is done by phone, PDF and CA calls at year end and before GST filing. It is boring, important and safe, and it pulls the other business onto Smara.

IndiaMART: help sellers win leads (do not copy IndiaMART)

  • For paid IndiaMART sellers: Smara pulls new leads through IndiaMART's own API and drafts a reply with price and stock from the owner's data. The owner taps, and the buyer gets an answer in minutes instead of hours.
  • That is real value (more leads won) using an official route, with no scraping. Build it once 2–3 pilot users are IndiaMART sellers.

Banswara's mix of synthetic yarn, textiles, marble and agriculture is from the DC-MSME district profile and the ODOP page.

How to win trust in a small city

  • Go through the local CA. One CA has 50–200 business clients and is already trusted with their books. Offer CAs a free dashboard of their clients' receivables, then a referral share.
  • Sit with the owner for 20 minutes, in person. Import their sheet together and show their top 10 dues on WhatsApp. The "wow" is seeing their own customers' names.
  • Never send anything to their customers without them. Say this first. It is the number one fear.
  • Weekly 5-minute check-in call for the first month. Turn every complaint into a fix and a test.

What to measure every week (this is also your grant evidence)

  • ₹ collected after Smara reminders (from the ledger: invoices marked paid after a reminder).
  • Average days from due date to payment, before and after.
  • Weekly active owners, and approvals per owner.
  • Hours saved, asked directly: "How many calls did you not make this week?"
  • Cost per shop from the usage report.

9. Next 30 days

  1. Meta: get a real business number, start business verification, get one template set approved (v1 or v2).
  2. List 60 Banswara businesses across the five segments, plus 3 CAs. Visit 10 a week.
  3. Onboard the first 5 in person. Import real files, fix every import bug the same day, and confirm totals.
  4. Add a weekly "result card" for each owner on WhatsApp: "This week: ₹X collected after reminders, Y promises tracked, Z calls saved". This is the feature that sells, and it is small to build.
  5. Start the iStart application with pilot numbers; register for Startup India recognition (needed for SISFS).
  6. Design "Khata Milao" (ledger match between two Smara users) and ship it once 2 pilot users trade with each other.

The one thing to remember: win Banswara with vasooli that saves real rupees, make every result visible in a weekly WhatsApp card, and let the network grow from the ledgers your users already share with each other. The agent-to-agent B2B channel is where this ends up, not where it starts.
